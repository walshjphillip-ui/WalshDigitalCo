Plain answers

What you're actually buying.

Every one of these gets asked on the first call. None of it is complicated once somebody explains it without the jargon.

Do you run ads?

No. None of this is ad management — no Google Ads, no Meta ads, no ad budget passing through the studio. When people hear "digital marketing" they usually picture exactly that, and it isn't what this is.

The difference is worth understanding, because it's the whole argument. Ads rent attention: you pay, people arrive, and the moment you stop paying it stops with it. A digital presence is what people find when they go looking for you on their own — the website, the brand, the Google listing, the feed, the reviews. That keeps working whether or not you're spending this month, and it compounds instead of resetting.

If ads are genuinely what your business needs, I'll say so rather than sell you something else. But ads pointed at a weak presence just cost more per customer — people click, look, and leave. Fixing what they land on usually comes first.

What is SEO, actually?

It's the work that makes you the result someone finds when they search for what you do, near where you are. There's no trick to it — it's four ordinary things done properly: building the site so Google can read it, using the words your customers actually type, filling out your Google Business Profile completely, and keeping reviews coming in.

What it isn't: paying Google for ads. That's separate, and it stops the moment you stop paying. SEO compounds instead.

What's the difference between Basic, Full and Advanced SEO?

Basic is the foundation — the site built and labelled correctly, submitted to Google, so you can be found at all.

Full adds the local work: your Google Business Profile properly set up, the terms people in your area actually search, a page for each place you serve, and monthly tracking of where you rank.

Advanced adds the ongoing push — regular content aimed at what you want to rank for, watching what competitors are doing, and tuning the pages that turn visitors into calls.

What's actually included in branding?

A logo, a colour palette, the typefaces, and the rules for how they go together — then those applied everywhere they show up.

The point isn't the logo on its own. It's that your website, your feed, your van, your invoice and your front window all look like the same business. That consistency is most of what makes a small operation read as established.

What are the 15 videos a month?

Short vertical videos for Instagram, TikTok and Facebook — filmed, cut, captioned and colour-graded, delivered finished. On Growth and Premium they're posted for you as well, so nothing sits in a folder.

They come from a shoot day at your business. One session generally produces enough material for the month.

What does social media management involve?

Deciding what to post and when, writing the captions, making the graphics, scheduling it and putting it out — on two platforms with Growth, three or more with Premium.

Premium also covers engagement: replying to comments and messages, so the account is actually attended rather than just fed.

What's included with the website?

Design and build from scratch, hosting, your domain connected, the security certificate, ongoing updates and backups. Not a template with your name dropped into it.

On Growth the updates are ongoing and animation is included; on Premium those updates are prioritised and you get the functional pieces built for your trade — booking, payments, quote generation.

What is "content strategy" — and how is it different from content?

Content is the thing itself: the video, the photo, the post. Strategy is deciding what's worth making in the first place — what your customers respond to, what you're trying to be known for, and what to put out when.

Without it you get a feed that's busy but going nowhere in particular.

How much of my time does this take?

A fifteen-minute call at the start, a shoot day when we make the content, and approvals as things go out. That's the intent of the whole arrangement — you run the business, the studio runs the presence.

Can I change plans, and is there a contract?

Every plan is month to month. Move up or down whenever it suits — most businesses start on Growth and adjust once they see what they're using.

What happens to my website if I stop the subscription?

Your brand and your media are yours — the logo, the identity, the photography and the video were made for your business and you keep them.

The website itself runs on the studio's hosting as part of the subscription, so it stops being served when the subscription ends. If you'd rather take it with you, say so and we'll arrange it rather than leave you stuck.
