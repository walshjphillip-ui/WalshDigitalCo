Sound familiar?

People find you. Then they pick someone else.

Most local businesses aren't short on skill — they're losing customers in the gap between someone looking them up and someone picking up the phone. It usually looks like this.

Outdated website

“Our website was fine… about six years ago.”

People judge the business by the site in seconds. Dated reads as closed, cheap or careless — even when you're none of those.

Visitors don't convert

“People visit the site, then call someone else.”

No clear offer, no obvious next step, a number buried in the footer. Interest cools before anyone reaches you.

Invisible locally

“We don't show up when people search nearby.”

If you're not in the map results for your trade and your town, most searchers never scroll far enough to find you.

Stale reviews

“Our last Google review is from two years ago.”

Recent, answered reviews tip a stranger into calling. Old ones quietly send them to whoever looks busier.

Inconsistent social

“Our Instagram hasn't been touched since spring.”

A quiet feed makes people wonder if you're still open. They won't ask — they'll just book elsewhere.

No time for content

“I know we should post more. I'm running a business.”

Content is a job on its own. Squeezed in after hours, it happens in bursts and then stops.

Weak branding

“The logo, the site and the feed look like three different companies.”

Inconsistency makes a solid business look smaller and newer than it really is.

Competitors look better

“The place down the road looks twice our size online.”

Customers can't judge your work before they buy. They judge what they can see — and right now that favours them.

None of this is about taste, and all of it is fixable. The free audit shows which of these are costing you — and which one to fix first.
